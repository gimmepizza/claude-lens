Biological Brains vs LLMs — One Honest Axis

Placing large language models beside biological nervous systems on the only defensible shared scale: learned connections — synapses in a brain, parameters in a model. The result overturns the usual "AI already beats the brain" story, and points to what actually transcends the divide.

The unit rule (why this isn't the usual comparison)
  • Parameters ↔ synapses, never parameters ↔ neurons. Both are the learned connection weights — the thing that stores what the system knows. This is the one axis where the comparison is fair.
  • An LLM "neuron" is not a neuron (no dendrites, spikes, or neuromodulation), so neuron-count comparisons are excluded here.
  • No single "intelligence" score. LLMs are superhuman at some tasks and absent capacities (embodiment, continual learning) at others — a single number would mislead. Capability is discussed, not plotted.
  • Numbers are best-estimates. Closed-model parameters (esp. GPT-4) are unconfirmed; synapse counts are order-of-magnitude. Everything is on log scales.
Theme

1 · Where LLMs actually sit, by connection count

Every system on one log axis of learned connections (biological synapses / model parameters), sorted. Frontier LLMs interleave around the mouse — roughly 100–1000× below the human brain. Impressive competence at small-mammal connectivity, reached by a different route.

2 · The scaling trajectory vs biological reference lines

LLM parameter counts over time (log). Even the steep climb sits between the mouse and human synapse lines — the trajectory is approaching small-mammal scale, not human scale.

3 · Data efficiency — the largest divergence

Language a system sees to become fluent (log). A human: ~10⁸ words by adulthood. An LLM: 10¹²–10¹³ tokens — 10,000–100,000× more. Whatever the brain does, it is not this.

4 · Energy — the second-largest divergence
