The Progression of Brain Complexity

Brain "complexity" is not one number. These charts treat it as a multi-dimensional quality function across 19 animals — and show that which animal "leads" depends entirely on which dimension you weight. There is no single ladder.

Assumptions & how to read this
  • Complexity is a vector. Six dimensions are used: brain mass, total neurons, associative-forebrain neurons (cortex/pallium; central-brain estimate for invertebrates), encephalization quotient (EQ), neuron density, and a cognition score.
  • Numbers are best-estimates from the neuroscience literature (largely Herculano-Houzel neuron counts) with real uncertainty; one species stands in for a group. Values flagged ◆ uncertain in tooltips.
  • The cognition score (0–10) is subjective — assigned from documented capacities (tool use, self-recognition, planning, social learning, communication), not a measurement. Toggle it off to see rankings on hard numbers only.
  • Invertebrate counts are not strictly comparable to vertebrates (e.g. ~2/3 of octopus neurons are in its arms; "associative neurons" has no exact invertebrate equivalent).
  • Counts, mass, and density use log scales (the data spans 302 neurons to 257 billion — 9 orders of magnitude). Educational, not a definitive ranking.
Theme

1 · Every animal as a profile

Parallel coordinates: each line is one animal traced across all dimensions (higher = more). Crossing lines mean the rankings disagree — the visual signature of "no single ladder." Hover a line to isolate it.

2 · The two best cognition predictors

Associative-forebrain neurons vs encephalization quotient. Point size = total neurons. Clades cluster differently — primates and corvids reach high associative counts by different routes.

3 · Neurons vs brain size — and why birds break the trend

Total neurons against brain mass (log–log). Separate trend lines for mammals and birds: birds pack far more neurons into a gram of brain, which is how a raven rivals a monkey. The human point is marked.

4 · The ranking reshuffles with every dimension

The same 11 vertebrates ranked by five different quality functions. Follow any line: the elephant tops total neurons but falls on EQ and cognition; the human tops associative neurons and cognition but not mass. This is the core point.

5 · One composite index — and how fragile it is

A single weighted "complexity" score (normalized dimensions combined). Switch the weighting to watch the ranking rearrange — proof that any single number is a choice, not a fact.

Weighting

6 · Complexity in deep time

When key neural innovations first appeared (approximate ages; ordered oldest → most recent, not drawn to scale). "Progression" in the only sense that is real — the accumulation of architectural inventions over ~600 million years, not a rank of living species.

Sources & caveats. Neuron counts chiefly from Herculano-Houzel and colleagues (isotropic-fractionator studies); EQ from standard comparative tables; the cognition score is the author's documented-capacity composite (subjective). Figures are order-of-magnitude best-estimates and several (notably cetacean counts and avian EQ) are method-dependent.
